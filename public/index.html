SENTRYSHIELD

acoustic perception prototype

A phone emits near-ultrasonic chirps and listens for the echoes. On-device DSP turns each echo into a range, a tiny neural network guesses what the surface is, and a laptop fuses the stream into a live map of the space — built from sound rather than light.

speaker→17.5–22 kHz chirp→environment→microphone→matched filter→CFAR→range→EchoNet→temporal fusion→spatial fusion→environment map

No phone? No microphone? No AWS? The command center runs a deterministic digital twin that raycasts a virtual building and synthesises physically-modelled echoes, which the real classifier then labels. It emits the identical detection schema, so the map cannot tell the difference — and neither the demo nor the map needs credentials, hardware or a network to work.

Research prototype. Ranges are estimated from a single-element sensor with a wide beam; bearing is the phone's boresight, not a resolved angle; position is dead-reckoned, not surveyed; and the echo classifier scores 69.5 % on synthetic validation (WALL 91 %, SOFT 74 %, OPENING 43 %). Not a navigation or safety device, and no smoke-penetration or collision-avoidance claims are made.
